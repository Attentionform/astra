ASTra is licensed under the [Business Source License 1.1](LICENSE) (BSL). This page explains it in plain words. If this page and the [LICENSE](LICENSE) disagree, the LICENSE wins.

## In one minute

- You can read, run, modify and redistribute the source code.

A company you set up for your own work is free while it stays inside the small-business limits.

- **Your documents are yours.** The license puts no restriction on documents you create with ASTra.
- **Each version becomes Apache-2.0** four years after it is first made public.

ASTra is **source-available**. It is not "open source" in the sense of the Open Source Definition, because the free use is limited.

## Common questions

**I am a freelancer. Can I use ASTra for client work?** Yes. Using it to produce documents for clients is covered. You pay nothing, and you do not need to tell anyone.

**My startup is small. Do I need to do anything?** No. If you are under both limits you may use ASTra in production and in your own product. Keep an eye on the limits.

**Can I build ASTra into my own product or service?** Yes, if your product has substantial functionality of its own and your business is under both limits (USD 1,000,000 revenue and 10 people, with affiliates). This applies to everyone, a solo person or a large company. Your customers use ASTra only as part of your product. At or above either limit you need an Embedded license. Using ASTra for your own work, such as reports, documentation, planning or client deliverables, is a different thing: that is free for individuals at any income.

**Can I make videos, stream, review or teach with ASTra?** Yes. Recording a tutorial, running a live stream, writing a review or teaching a course is fine, and it does not matter whether you earn money from the video or the course. A person working on their own account is free whatever they earn, and demonstrating the software is non-production use, which is free for everyone. Hosting ASTra as a service for your audience, or reselling it, is a competing offering and always needs a commercial license.

**Can I build an open-source project on ASTra?** Yes, if your project has substantial functionality of its own. Your own code can be under any license, such as MIT or Apache-2.0. You list ASTra as a dependency and you do not relicense it. People who use your project use ASTra only as part of it, which is covered when you are an individual or a small business, even if your project is free of charge. A company at or above the small-business limits needs a commercial license for its own production use of ASTra, and the people who use a project published by such a company are not covered by the rule above and need their own right to use ASTra. A thin wrapper whose value is mainly ASTra itself is a competing offering and needs a commercial license. If you copy ASTra source into your repository, keep ASTra's LICENSE with it: ASTra code cannot be relicensed as MIT or Apache-2.0 until its own Change Date. Some license scanners and software distributions do not treat the BSL as open source, so describe your project as "depends on ASTra (source-available)".

**What is not allowed without a license?** A "competing offering": a product or service offered to others whose value consists mainly of ASTra itself. It includes hosting ASTra as a service for others; making ASTra's operations, commands, API or tools available to third parties as a service in itself, directly or through a thin interface (for example a public API or MCP endpoint whose main purpose is access to ASTra); a desktop, web or other editor whose value is mainly ASTra's document functionality; and selling ASTra, or a product or service whose value is mainly ASTra, with or without another name. A product that uses ASTra to implement substantial features of its own is not a competing offering. That includes an AI agent or an agent platform that uses ASTra as one of its tools: it is a product, free while your business is under the limits, and it needs an Embedded license after that. You may copy, modify and redistribute ASTra free of charge, modified or not, provided every copy keeps the LICENSE and the copyright and license notices. Three tests decide it. It is a competing offering if most of what customers pay for or use it for is ASTra's document functionality; if little would remain without ASTra (an interface, login, billing or prompts around ASTra's operations are not features of your own); or if its main purpose is to let third parties reach ASTra's operations, commands, API or tools. A competing offering always needs a commercial license, whatever your size.

**Can I ask before I build?** Yes. Describe the product to astra@attentionform.com and we aim to tell you in writing within five working days whether it is a competing offering. This is a target, not a promise of a deadline.

**Do the limits include my parent company and affiliates?** Yes. Revenue and headcount are counted for your whole group: companies that control you, companies you control, and companies under the same control. A separate company set up to stay under the limits does not count as separate. An investor that holds 50% or less and has only customary minority rights, such as a board seat or consent over a financing or a sale, does not make its other portfolio companies count together with you.

**How many people count?** Employees, owners and partners who work in the business, and regular contractors. Founders count. Revenue in another currency is converted into US dollars at the exchange rate on the last day of your fiscal year.

**Can my company try ASTra for as long as it likes?** Yes, on sample documents, with no time limit. Building and testing an integration and demonstrating it are always free. Running your company's real work through it, even in a pilot, is production use and needs a license if your company is above the small-business limits. If a pilot is going well, write to us: we answer quickly.

**Does my open-source project's CI need a license?** No. Running your project's tests is non-production use and is free for everyone. Using ASTra to process real documents in an organization's regular operations is production use. People who run your project in production need their own right to use ASTra.

**If I publish a free product that includes ASTra, do my users need a license?** If you are an individual or a small business and your product has substantial functionality of its own, your users may use ASTra as part of your product, whatever their size. If the product's value is mainly ASTra, it is a competing offering and needs a commercial license. If you are a company above the small-business limits, you need a commercial license, and your users are not covered by the rule above.

**What if I stop qualifying?** You can continue your existing use for 90 days, then you need a commercial license or you stop. A small business stops qualifying on the first day after the fiscal year in which it reached a limit. Example: a company passes USD 1,000,000 in July 2024 with a calendar fiscal year. It stays free through 2024, stops qualifying on 1 January 2025, and has until about 31 March 2025 to buy.

**Do I have to publish my own code?** No. The BSL has no copyleft and no obligation to share your code.

**Is the license going to get stricter?** The terms of a released version never become stricter. A later version may carry different terms. Version 0.3.0 closes some gaps in the free-use rules. Every version released before 0.3.0 keeps its original terms.

**Can I send a pull request?** Yes. Sign the [contributor license agreement](CLA.md) once, by adding one small signature file to your first pull request. See [CONTRIBUTING.md](CONTRIBUTING.md).

**What about the names and logos?** They are covered separately: see [TRADEMARKS.md](TRADEMARKS.md).

**What about third-party code inside ASTra?** It keeps its own license. See [THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md](THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md). A folder that has its own license file is under that license.

## Open-source projects

The MIT, Apache or other license on your project's own code does not change ASTra's license. ASTra stays under its own license, and each person's use of it is judged by that person's own category.

- **An individual maintainer, a qualifying non-profit or foundation, a school:** free.
- **A company or business behind the project:** free while it is under USD 1,000,000 revenue and 10 people, counted with affiliates. At or above either limit, that business needs a commercial license (Embedded, if the project is its product).
- **ASTra bundled in the project:** users may use ASTra only as part of the project, for as long as the maker qualifies or holds the license.
- **Users install ASTra themselves:** ASTra is not part of the project, so each user's use is governed by that user's own category.
- **Forks and redistribution:** you may copy, modify and redistribute ASTra free of charge, provided every copy keeps the LICENSE and the copyright and license notices. Selling ASTra itself, or a product or service that is mainly ASTra, is a competing offering.

Trying ASTra on a few of your real files, to see whether it works for you, is free for everyone. It becomes production use when you rely on the results in your regular operations.

## For maintainers

- The `LICENSE` file describes the Licensed Work as every version the Licensor makes publicly available, so it needs no edit per release. Each version's Change Date is four years after the day it was first made public: write that day next to the version in `CHANGELOG.md` when you release.
- The `LICENSE` in the root and in each package must stay identical. `tests/unit/license-consistency.test.ts` checks this.
- Use the SPDX id `BUSL-1.1` in `package.json`.
- Never describe ASTra as "open source". Say "source-available".
